The more responsibility I've taken on, the less useful I've found anxiety to be. Not less *present* — less useful. It's worth being precise about the difference.

Anxiety is old hardware. It evolved to detect movement in the bushes and prime you to run. The detection system still works; the threats changed. Now it fires on ambiguous Slack messages and industry headlines, and because there's nothing to physically run from, the energy has nowhere to go. So it loops.

That's the key observation: anxiety without an available action is just a process spinning at 100% CPU, producing nothing.

### The reframe that actually works

Most advice about anxiety is about suppressing the signal. I think that's backwards. The signal is fine — it's *unrouted*.

Anxiety and excitement are nearly identical physiological states. The difference is direction. Excitement has a target; anxiety doesn't. So the fix isn't "calm down," it's "pick a target": what is the one concrete thing I can do about this today?

If the answer exists, do it, and the loop usually terminates. If the answer genuinely doesn't exist — it's outside your control — then you've learned something more valuable: this thread can be killed entirely.

Two things kill most of my anxiety before it starts: planning done in advance, and identity questions resolved in advance. If you already know what you're optimising for, very few surprises are actually surprising.

### A live example: AI

The current test case is AI. Half the people I know in design and engineering are running the loop: *what happens to my job, my skills, my mortgage?*

Same signal, two routings.

Routed as worry, it produces nothing — you refresh the news and feel worse. Routed as action, the exact same uncertainty reads as the largest window of opportunity in a generation: to build, to start something, to create value directly, and to end up more economically independent than the old career ladder ever allowed.

This isn't naive optimism. Moments of disruption are precisely when the world is still soft enough to shape. Stability is when everything is already decided by someone else.

### Control what you can

My working policy is boring and effective: ignore what I can't influence, invest everything in what I can — skills, output, standards, and who I work with.

Optimism, in this framing, isn't a temperament. It's a strategy: a deliberate bias toward acting wherever action is possible.

When the ground moves, you can freeze or you can move with it. The signal you feel in that moment is the same either way. What you do with it is the whole game.
